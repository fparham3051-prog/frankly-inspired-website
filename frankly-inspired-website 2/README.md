# Frankly Inspired — Website (v1)

Mostly-single-page site built from `Frankly_Inspired_Website_Build_Kit`, kept to as few files as make sense so Brian isn't hunting across a large multi-page build for copy changes. `index.html` is the lean landing page/funnel; bio, experience, and resume content live on a second page, `about.html` (see "Landing page / About split" below).

## Fixes from the founder's critique pass

`Frankly_Inspired_Founder_Critique_and_Roadmap.md` (one level up, in the main outputs folder) is a full write-up of an accessibility/UX/business critique of this site. The concrete, code-level fixes from that critique's "Where to Focus First" list — plus a couple of related ones found while implementing it — are done:

- **Gold text-contrast fix.** `var(--gold)` on small/regular text (link hovers, `.role-meta`, the Engagement Model step numbers, status messages) measured 3.07:1 on white — under the 4.5:1 WCAG AA requirement. A new `--gold-accessible` (`#8E7134`, 4.60:1) now covers every one of those spots. Large text (prices, the assessment's big score number) was already fine at 3:1 and keeps the brighter `--gold`.
- **"More" → "About."** The nav dropdown trigger now says what's actually behind it instead of a content-free label.
- **Heading hierarchy fixed in Consulting.** Pillar card titles, pricing card titles, and the assessment callout heading were all H3s sitting flat under one H2 — a screen reader had no way to tell "section header" from "card title." They're now H4s nested under the real H3 subsection headers (Service Pillars, Engagement Model, Engagement Options). `h4` was added to the base heading rule so nothing visually changed except the semantics.
- **Scroll-reveal safety net.** Every page now sets `class="no-js"` on `<html>`, flipped to `class="js"` by a one-line inline script at the very top of `<head>`. The `.reveal` hiding CSS is now gated behind `html.js`, so if JavaScript is ever disabled, content simply stays visible — no dependency on `js/main.js` running at all. On top of that, `js/main.js` now force-reveals anything still hidden after 2.5 seconds, as a backstop against the observer missing an element for any reason.
- **Pricing button / lead-routing mismatch.** The Implementation Retainer's button only pre-checked "Organizational Development and Operations" despite its own bullet list spanning sustainability, revenue, operations, *and* leadership. `data-plan` now accepts a comma-separated list (`js/main.js`'s click handler splits and checks each one), and both retainer buttons pre-check every service area their plan actually covers.
- **"Best fit" line on each pricing card.** One sentence per plan (advisory-only vs. full implementation vs. single project), pulled directly from each plan's own existing bullet list — not invented org-size or budget figures, which the site's data doesn't have and shouldn't guess at.

Left alone, deliberately: the "& Associates" naming question and the full spacing-scale cleanup are both flagged in the critique doc as decisions/undertakings for Franklin, not fixes to make unilaterally — see that document for the reasoning.

## Landing page / About split

The landing page had grown long enough (Hero, Positioning, Impact Metrics, Philosophy, About, Consulting, Experience, Speaking, Resume, Closing CTA, Contact) that it felt bulky rather than funnel-shaped. To lean it out, everything that's background information rather than a step in "should I contact Franklin" was moved off `index.html` onto a new page, `about.html`:

- **Stayed on `index.html`:** Hero, Positioning (with a "More about Franklin's background" link to `about.html`), the "How Franklin Helps" teaser, Consulting (Service Pillars, Engagement Model, Pricing, assessment callout), Contact.
- **Moved to `about.html`:** the Philosophy quote, About, Experience & Impact (including the Impact Metrics stat grid), Speaking, and Resume — in that order, ending with its own Closing CTA back into the funnel so the page doesn't dead-end.

The primary nav now has three top-level links (Consulting, Free Assessment, Contact) plus a **"More" dropdown** for About / Experience & Impact / Speaking / Resume, instead of seven flat links. On desktop it's a hover/click panel; on mobile (inside the hamburger menu) it becomes an inline accordion, since dropdown panels don't work well on touch. The toggle logic lives in `js/main.js` under "5. 'More' nav dropdown." `about.html` is also linked from the footer ("About & Experience") on every page for anyone who wants it without using the nav.

Nothing about the Self-Assessment or Engagement Tracker changed — the five `id="pillar-*"` anchors that the assessment results page links to are still on `index.html`'s Consulting section, untouched.

## Scroll reveal

Text and cards throughout `index.html` and `about.html` now fade and slide up into place the first time they scroll into view, instead of the whole page just being static. It's built with `class="reveal"` on the elements that animate, plain CSS transitions (`.reveal` / `.reveal.is-visible` in `styles.css`), and an `IntersectionObserver` in `js/main.js` ("6. Scroll reveal") that adds `.is-visible` once and never re-triggers on repeat scrolling. Elements that share a parent (card grids, pricing cards, stat grids) get a small staggered delay so they cascade in rather than all popping in at once.

Two things worth knowing if you're editing copy:
- The whole system is wrapped in a `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` CSS check — visitors who've told their OS they want less motion just see the content directly, with no animation and nothing hidden.
- The Contact form fields are deliberately **not** wrapped in `reveal` — only the heading and intro above the form are, so nothing about filling out or tabbing through the form is affected by the animation.

## "Mission-driven institutions" as the standard phrase

The site used to describe Franklin's clients with a few different near-synonyms scattered around — "mission-driven organizations," "mission-driven leaders," plain "nonprofit(s)" — rather than one consistent phrase. Franklin asked whether these should all be spelled out explicitly (institutions, nonprofits, mission-driven organizations) or whether one catch-all term would serve better; the latter, since his actual client base is broader than "nonprofit" alone (higher ed, professional associations, and nonprofits proper), and listing near-synonyms together reads as padding rather than precision.

Franklin chose **"mission-driven institutions"** as the standard phrase — it also ties back to how he already describes himself ("institution builder"). It's now used consistently in the Hero subhead, the Positioning lede, and the Consulting section note on `index.html`. "Nonprofit" was deliberately left in place in the two spots doing SEO/categorization work — the H1 ("Building stronger nonprofits...") and the page `<meta name="description">` — since that's still the term people actually search for. The Contact/Closing CTA refrain ("Strong missions need sustainable institutions") and the About page's philosophy quote were left as-is; they're intentional turns of phrase rather than the enumerated-list pattern Franklin was asking about.

No separate price point was added — it's covered under the existing retainer/project pricing, same as AI Advisory. It is a new option in the Contact form's "How can I support your organization?" list ("1:1 Leadership Coaching"), so inbound interest in coaching specifically is trackable even though the marketing copy doesn't break it out as its own service.

To add the effect to a new element, just add `class="reveal"` (or append `reveal` to its existing class list) — no other markup changes needed.

## Self-assessment tool

`assessment.html` is the "Nonprofit Sustainability Self-Assessment" built as a longer-term way to show real impact data (see the Client Intake & Outcomes Framework doc, delivered alongside this site, for the reasoning). It's 15 questions across the same five pillars as the Consulting section, scored 3-15 each (composite 15-75), and shows an instant results breakdown plus a priority-area callout — all calculated in the browser. Each pillar in the results links directly to that specific service on `index.html` (see `id="pillar-*"` on the Service Pillar cards), so a visitor's weakest area points straight at the matching service instead of a generic page. The optional "email me my results" box, and the main Contact form, both post to `frankly-inspired-backend` (see that folder's README for deployment status).

It's linked from three places on the main site: the primary nav (both desktop and mobile), a callout box in the Consulting section right at the pricing decision point ("not ready for a conversation yet? take the free assessment first" — a lower-commitment path for visitors who aren't ready to book a call), and the footer.

If clients start actually taking the assessment, keep its question wording and scoring in sync with the Engagement Tracker's baseline columns (both companion files) — they're deliberately built to the same 3-15-per-pillar scale so the two data sources stay comparable.

### AI-generated result summary

After a visitor submits the assessment, `js/assessment.js` shows the deterministic score, band, and pillar breakdown instantly, then makes a background call to `frankly-inspired-backend`'s `/api/assessment-narrative` endpoint for a short, personalized read of their results, written by Claude. The static band description is shown immediately and only gets replaced if the AI call succeeds — if it's slow, fails, or the backend has no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` configured, the visitor never sees an error, they just keep the static text. See `frankly-inspired-backend/README.md`, "AI narrative," for the prompt itself and why it can't invent facts about a visitor's organization.

## Service Pillars consolidation (3 headline offerings)

A competitive-landscape review of comparable nonprofit-management consulting sites (research summarized in the chat response this change shipped with) found that peer firms typically lead with 2–4 clear offerings, not five-plus. The Consulting section's card grid was consolidated from five cards to three to match that pattern and reduce a visitor's cognitive load:

- **Sustainability Strategy** — unchanged.
- **Revenue & Growth** — merges the former "Revenue Diversification" and "Partnership & Ecosystem Strategy" cards.
- **Leadership & Operations** — merges the former "Leadership & Organizational Development" and "Operations & Infrastructure" cards, and now also carries a short AI-readiness mention (see above).

This is a marketing-presentation change only. The underlying five-dimension measurement — the Self-Assessment's 15 questions and the Engagement Tracker's baseline columns — is untouched and still scores Sustainability Strategy, Revenue Diversification, Leadership & Organizational Development, Operations & Infrastructure, and Partnership & Ecosystem Strategy as five separate pillars. To keep the assessment's per-pillar result links working, all five original `id="pillar-*"` anchors still exist in `index.html`: two of them (`pillar-partnership`, `pillar-operations`) are invisible anchor spans (`.anchor-offset` in `styles.css`) sitting inside the merged cards, so a link to any of the five still lands on the right part of the page.

The homepage teaser ("How Franklin Helps") and the Speaking & Advisory section were both trimmed to match: the teaser now shows 3 tags instead of 5, and Speaking dropped its "Potential Speaking Themes" list down to a single low-key line (its `id="speaking"` anchor and nav link still work).

## Cookie consent banner and analytics

Every page (`index.html`, `assessment.html`, `privacy.html`, `terms.html`) now loads `js/cookie-consent.js`, which shows a bottom banner on a visitor's first visit asking them to Accept or Decline optional cookies. Their choice is remembered in `localStorage` (not itself a cookie) so the banner doesn't reappear on later visits, and a "Manage Cookie Preferences" link in the footer reopens it if they want to change their mind.

`js/analytics.js` is the on/off switch for Google Analytics. It currently does nothing — `GA_MEASUREMENT_ID` at the top of that file is blank. **When you're ready to turn on analytics:** get your GA4 Measurement ID (looks like `G-XXXXXXXXXX`) from Google Analytics and paste it into that one variable. From that moment on, the script loads automatically, but only for visitors who click "Accept" on the banner — nobody who declines, or hasn't answered yet, gets a tracking script loaded. No other code changes needed.

The Privacy Policy's "Website Analytics and Cookies" section was updated to describe this consent-gated behavior accurately.
