Annual Institutional Health report · 2026 edition

DMV Nonprofit Health Brief

We read the public Form 990 filings of 1,668 education, human services, youth, and workforce nonprofits across DC, Maryland, and Virginia. Most are growing. More than a third of those growing are doing it on a foundation that isn't keeping up.

Five findings

38%of organizations that grew revenue 20% or more show strain: reserves under three months, a deficit with spending outrunning revenue, or reserves that fell sharply.
52%saw expenses grow faster than revenue over the five-year period.
39%ran a deficit in their most recent filed year.
23%have net assets covering less than three months of expenses (11% less than one month).
37%have a thinner cushion than five years earlier, measured in months of expenses.

What it means

Growth is the common story here: 57% of these organizations grew revenue by at least 20% over the period, and only 17% shrank by 10% or more. The problem is rarely demand. It's that programs expand faster than the systems, reserves, and leadership capacity that have to carry them.

Strain shows up first in the numbers a funder reads: a deficit year, a reserve that drops, expenses that grow a step ahead of revenue. Behind those numbers is usually one binding constraint, such as decisions that still route to one person, a site that runs on someone's memory, or outcomes no one can show. That's the constraint worth finding before the next grant or the next site.

About half (49%) rely on contributions and grants for 70% or more of revenue, so a single funder decision can move the whole picture.

How we did this

This is the first edition of an annual report, refreshed each fall as new filings are released. Published November 2026.

  • Who's included: tax-exempt organizations in DC, Maryland, and Virginia classified as education, human services, youth development, or employment, with annual revenue between about $500,000 and $20 million on the IRS master file, and at least one e-filed Form 990 with figures. 1,668 organizations met that bar.
  • Source: filed Form 990 summaries through ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer. For 96% of organizations the latest filing covers 2023 or 2024; the starting year is usually 2019. Public filings run one to two years behind.
  • Months of expenses in net assets: end-of-year net assets divided by one month of total expenses. Net assets include restricted funds and buildings, so this overstates the cash an organization can actually spend. Treat it as an upper bound.
  • Growers under strain: among organizations whose revenue grew 20% or more, the share with net assets under three months, a deficit year where expenses grew faster than revenue, or net assets that fell by 40% or more (in months) to below six months.
  • Privacy: this brief reports totals only. It doesn't name, rank, or describe any individual organization.
