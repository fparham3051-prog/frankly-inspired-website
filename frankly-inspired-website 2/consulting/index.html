Strategy for organizations that want to last.

Frankly Inspired and Associates, LLC works with mission-driven institutions that need more than a short-term fix. Engagements are designed around the organization's context, capacity, leadership needs, and sustainability goals.

Who we work with

The organization

  • Youth, education, workforce, and human services nonprofits in DC, Maryland, and Virginia
  • Annual revenue of roughly $500,000 to $20 million
  • Growing, or under pressure to: more programs, more sites, larger funders
  • A team big enough that the executive director can no longer hold every decision, but without a full layer of senior managers

The moment

  • A second site, city, or program is on the table
  • A larger funder or a multi-year grant is coming up
  • Everything still runs through the founder or executive director
  • Staff grew, but the strain didn't go down
  • A leadership transition is likely in the next two years

Probably not a fit

  • You need a grant writer, bookkeeper, or attorney (we'll refer you)
  • You want a full strategic plan written for you
  • There's no decision ahead that the answer would change

Our DMV Nonprofit Health Brief shows why this group: 38% of growing nonprofits in this range show financial strain while they grow.

What happens before, during, and after the Diagnostic

  1. Before
    A free 30-minute conversation. We talk about the decision in front of you, by when, and who decides. If a Diagnostic isn't the right step, I'll say so. If it is, you get a one-page proposal and engagement letter within a few days.
  2. Week 1
    Kickoff. An intake with the executive director (and board chair, if possible). We request the evidence: 990s, budget, policies, org chart, and how work actually runs. A six-minute leader survey goes to the executive director, board, and senior staff.
  3. Weeks 2 to 3
    Scoring on evidence. Each of the seven areas is scored on what can be shown, with a note explaining every score. Gaps between what leaders say and what the documents show are flagged.
  4. Week 4
    Readout. A 60 to 90 minute session with your leadership: the scorecard, the memo naming the one gap that will break first (and the runner-up), and the 90-Day Improvement Plan. Your private client portal opens the same day.
  5. After
    The plan runs. Your team logs a 15-minute weekly check-in in the portal. The 90-day review and the six-month reassessment are booked before we close, so you can see what actually moved. Continuing support is optional, never assumed.

What's done by day 30, 60, and 90

By day 30

  • Diagnostic complete: scorecard, memo, and readout
  • The gap to fix first named and agreed by leadership
  • A 90-Day Improvement Plan: one target and three to six actions, each with an owner and a date
  • Client portal live, weekly check-ins started

By day 60

  • First actions finished, with the evidence logged
  • A month-one check: does the diagnosis still hold?
  • Anything blocked unblocked or re-planned
  • A progress view you can share with your board

By day 90

  • The 90-day review: what moved, shown with evidence, not impressions
  • The change written down, owned, and taught to staff
  • The next constraint in line, and a decision: next plan, retainer, or close out
  • Six-month reassessment on the calendar

What the price is weighed against

The Diagnostic is priced against the decision it informs, not the hours it takes. A flat $2,000 to $2,500 is small next to what it protects:

  • Expansion: opening a second site before the first one runs without you can cost a year of operating budget and staff you can't keep.
  • A major grant: a declined multi-year application, or one awarded and then poorly reported, shapes how that funder sees you for years.
  • Hiring: adding staff to fix what is really a decision-rights or workflow problem adds payroll without adding capacity.
  • Leadership transition: relationships and knowledge that live in one person leave with that person.

That's also why the fee is fixed and the scope is defined: it's an easy, bounded request for your board or a funder to approve.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents will I need to share?

For Grant Readiness, financial health is scored from your public IRS Form 990 filings, so you don't need to send them. For any diagnostic, you can choose to share governance policies, board materials, financial statements, or strategic plans to support the evidence review.

How is my organization's information handled?

What you share is used only to complete your diagnostic. It's never sold or reused for another engagement without your knowledge, and you can ask for shared source materials to be deleted once the engagement ends. See the Privacy Policy.

Is this legal or accounting advice?

No. Where a finding touches legal, accounting, or safeguarding matters, you'll get a referral instead of a substitute opinion: a nonprofit CPA for financial controls and audits, an attorney for legal structure and compliance, a program evaluator for formal impact studies. We'll also tell you when not to proceed yet, for example with an expansion or a major application.

We're not ready to pay for a diagnostic. Where should we start?

Take one of the free tools. The 7-minute self-assessment covers all seven dimensions and points you to your likely constraint. Every tool you take after that adds to your Health Index, one profile you can print for your board.

How do I make the case to my board?

Print your free Health Index and bring it to your next board meeting. It shows all seven dimensions on one page, along with your likely constraint. Your board chair is welcome at the diagnostic's readout session. Some funders also offer capacity-building grants that can cover outside consulting, so ask your program officers.

How are the free tools scored?

Every tool uses the same seven dimensions and the same evidence rubric, from "not in place" to "verified." Each question and its research source is published on How Scoring Works.

Can a funder pay for the Diagnostic?

Yes. Many funders support capacity building, and the fixed price makes it an easy request. Use the sponsorship letter, and the invoice can go directly to the funder.
