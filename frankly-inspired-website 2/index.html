What the Research Shows

The First-Gift Cliff

Nationally, roughly 4 in 5 first-time donors never make a second gift: first-time donor retention sits around 18%, well below the 43% overall average. A structured stewardship process in a donor's first 90 days is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-cost fixes most nonprofits have available.

Fundraising Effectiveness Project, Association of Fundraising Professionals & GivingTuesday

See where your organization stands →

The Recurring-Giving Advantage

After one year, 71% of monthly donors are still giving, compared to just 24% of new one-time donors, a gap wide enough to make recurring giving one of the highest-leverage levers most nonprofits underuse. Converting even a portion of one-time donors into monthly sustainers does more for long-term revenue stability than acquiring new donors at the same rate.

M+R Benchmarks, an annual digital fundraising study

See where your organization stands →

The Planned-Giving Gap

Charitable bequests have made up roughly 7-9% of all U.S. giving in most years over the past four decades, and reached just over 10% in 2025, yet most nonprofits still don't have even a basic planned-giving program. The gap matters beyond the eventual gift: at one studied institution, realized bequest gifts averaged 21 times a donor's lifetime annual giving, meaning a planned-giving conversation is also a marker of a donor's deepest capacity, not just a future line item.

Giving USA; the Chronicle of Philanthropy; PG Calc planned-giving research

How We Work Together

Every engagement starts the same way: a discovery conversation, followed by the InstitutionalOS Diagnostic scored for your organization's actual lifecycle stage. From there, the method never changes, whether the priority is formation, funding, or long term durability.

  1. Assess. Structured scoring across the dimensions that matter at your stage, never a generic checklist.
  2. Verify. Higher scores require evidence: a documented policy, an observed practice, a filed financial statement, not self reported optimism. A strong score means something because it had to be earned.
  3. Decide. Every organization has one binding constraint right now, the single thing limiting progress more than any other. Naming it specifically is the job, not producing a wall of generic recommendations.
  4. Improve. A 90 day target for closing a meaningful share of the gap, concrete enough to act on Monday morning.
  5. Reassess. Your score is tracked over time through InstitutionalOS, so progress is measured, not just described.

This is the same method behind every free scorecard on this site. A paid diagnostic goes further: it verifies the evidence directly with you and ends in a working session, not just a number.

Organization: Sample Community Youth Alliance (fictional, for illustration only)
Pathway: Sustain (Institutional Durability) Diagnostic
Candid Seal today: Silver

  • 1. Strategy and Positioning (68/100). Written strategic plan in use; no written criteria for saying no to new opportunities.
  • 2. Program and Impact Evidence (52/100). Outputs tracked for every program; outcomes measured for only one.
  • 3. Revenue and Financial Health (58/100). Two years of filed financials reviewed; no reserve policy on file.
  • 4. Leadership and Organizational Capacity (44/100). Single point of failure identified in program leadership.
  • 5. Operations and Infrastructure (66/100). Core workflows documented; no KPI dashboard in use.
  • 6. Partnerships and Ecosystem (63/100). Strong partners, but only two of eight formalized.
  • 7. Governance and Risk (71/100). Bylaws and Form 990 policies current; succession plan not documented.

Constraint identified: Leadership bench strength is the binding constraint, not financial health, which is often assumed to be the problem.

90-day target: 44 to 58 (closing 25% of the gap to 100), via a documented succession plan for the top two program roles and one cross-trained backup per critical function.

Candid Seal step: Silver to Gold by uploading the latest filed Form 990 and completing the leader demographic fields, both already in hand.

What you get: the full scorecard above, a written memo explaining each score, and a 60-90 minute session walking through the plan, delivered whether the news is good or hard to hear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does working together start?

With a free 30-minute conversation, then the InstitutionalOS Diagnostic: $2,000 for Formation, $2,500 for Grant Readiness or Institutional Durability. It's the single entry point for every paid engagement.

What do I get from the diagnostic?

All seven Health Index dimensions scored against your stage's benchmarks, a written memo naming your single binding constraint, a 90-day plan, a live readout session, and ongoing access to track your score. See a sample.

What documents will I need to share?

For Grant Readiness, financial health is scored from your public IRS Form 990 filings, so you don't need to send them. For any diagnostic, you can choose to share governance policies, board materials, financial statements, or strategic plans to support the evidence review.

How is my organization's information handled?

What you share is used only to complete your diagnostic. It's never sold or reused for another engagement without your knowledge, and you can ask for shared source materials to be deleted once the engagement ends. See the Privacy Policy.

Is there a minimum commitment?

The advisory retainer has a three-month recommended minimum. Executive coaching is $350 per 60-minute session, scheduled as needed with no minimum.

Is this legal or accounting advice?

No. Where a finding touches legal, accounting, or safeguarding matters, you'll get a recommendation to bring in the right specialist rather than a substitute opinion.

We're not ready to pay for a diagnostic. Where should we start?

Take one of the free tools. The 7-minute self-assessment covers all seven dimensions and points you to your likely constraint. Every tool you take after that adds to your Health Index, one profile you can print for your board.

How do I make the case to my board?

Print your free Health Index and bring it to your next board meeting. It shows all seven dimensions on one page, along with your likely constraint. Your board chair is welcome at the diagnostic's readout session. Some funders also offer capacity-building grants that can cover outside consulting, so ask your program officers.

How are the free tools scored?

Every tool uses the same seven dimensions and the same evidence rubric, from "not in place" to "verified." Each question and its research source is published on How Scoring Works.
